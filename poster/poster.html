Brain inspired reactivation allows autonomous memory redistribution between predictive coding networks

Paul Saighi  ·  Université Paris Cité, CNRS, Integrative Neuroscience and Cognition Center (INCC), Paris  ·  paul.saighi.pro@gmail.com

1What sets reactivation priority?

  • Sleep reactivation redistributes representations from hippocampus to neocortex.
  • It is selective: new and weakly learned memories are reactivated more. Why?
  • CA1 recruits prefrontal cortex; independent prefrontal events suppress CA1.

Hypothesis: selective neocortical feedback steers reactivation toward what the neocortex has not yet learned.

$\varepsilon_{TS} = x_T - x_S$
$\tau_S \dot x_S = -\pi_S M_S^\top \varepsilon_S + \pi_{TS}\,\varepsilon_{TS}$
$\tau_T \dot x_T = -\pi_T M_T^\top \varepsilon_T + \cK\,\varepsilon_{TS} + \sigma_\xi \xi_T$
$\dot W_S = \eta_S\,\pi_S\,\varepsilon_S\, x_S^\top$

Reversed feedback sign: the student inhibits the teacher, like prefrontal suppression of CA1.

Storage: teacher clamped to patterns. Redistribution: no input, frozen teacher, student learns locally.

3Intrinsic activity alone redistributes memory content, one mode at a time

4Feedback climbs the student's surprise

$$q(x_T) = \min_{x_S} F_S(x_S, x_T)$$

$q$ is the student's surprise: the free energy left once it has settled on what the teacher reactivates.

$$\cK\,(x_T - x_S^*) = \frac{\cK}{\pi_{TS}}\,\nabla_{x_T} q$$

The feedback climbs that surprise: it pushes the teacher toward what the student explains worst.

5Same dynamics repair damage

After a lesion specific to digit 7, its error falls from 79 to 4 %. Nothing signals which memory was damaged.

Reactivation priority needs no scheduler or tag: it emerges from the hippocampal-neocortical mismatch.

Limits: linear rate units, shared code, one-to-one coupling. Sources: Tang 2023 · Wang, Poe & Zochowski 2008 · Schapiro 2018 · Giri 2019 · Wierzynski 2009 · Shin & Jadhav 2024
